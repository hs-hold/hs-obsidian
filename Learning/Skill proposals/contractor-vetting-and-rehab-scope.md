# Contractor vetting and rehab-scope checklist

## Why it helps H.S HOLDINGS
Turns contractor meetings and rehab bids into a fixed vetting and contract checklist, so MAO rehab numbers and flip/BRRRR timelines are based on real bids, not optimism.

## Suggested runner
A **new "Rehab Desk" bot** is the better long-term owner (bids, vetting files, draw schedule, punch list). Until that bot exists, the **secretary** can run the meeting checklist and **Deal Analysis** can apply the winning bid to MAO. Contract Desk may review the contractor agreement but is not the project manager.

## When to use
- A partner is about to meet or call contractors, trades, or a handyman in a target market.
- A deal is inside the inspection window and rehab cost must be confirmed.
- A contractor agreement or a draw request needs a checklist review.

## Build the bench
- Maintain more than one contractor; some will not answer. The training also lists trades separately: roofer, flooring, plumber, handyman, pool, and others. Agents and property-management companies are a different bench (`property-manager-vetting`).
- Prefer a contractor who can walk a house and give a usable rehab estimate quickly (the off-market class described a ~10-minute walk-through estimate as the standard for a core team member). That quick number is still a bid-to-be-confirmed, not a final contract price.
- Ask local realtors, other investors, and (once hired) a property manager for referrals.

## During the property inspection window
1. Get **three contractor bids (BIDs)** to validate the rehab number used in the offer.
2. Order a professional home inspection and add accepted findings to the contractor's initial bid.
3. Confirm funding is enough for **purchase plus rehab**, not purchase alone.
4. Interview the bidding contractors and choose one.
5. Prepare the contractor agreement before closing when possible, so work can start on day one.
6. Place insurance so it can be activated at closing. Compare multiple quotes, or use an agent who compares coverage, not just premium.

## Interview questions (for contractors who already sent a bid)
Cover at least:

- License and insurance (general liability and workers' comp), and who is on site versus subcontracted.
- Recent comparable jobs and a local reference (investor or realtor).
- How many jobs will run in parallel with yours.
- Proposed start and finish dates, work stages, and a payment / draw schedule.
- How change orders are priced and approved.
- Lien waiver / lien release practice at each draw and at final payment.
- Who pulls permits, and who meets inspectors.

## Contract terms to require (from the Cycle 28 rehab class)
Ask that the agreement include:

- Start and end dates, with a stage-by-stage schedule.
- Costs and payment timing by stage, after a supervisor or inspector signs off.
- A late-completion penalty.
- An exhibit listing materials, labor, and costs.
- A cost-overrun rule taught in class: **overruns above 15% are absorbed by the contractor** (confirm this is negotiated and legal in the contract; do not assume it is standard).
- Punch-list repairs at the end, required by the supervisor, at no added cost.
- Clear draw conditions and lien waivers.

## During the work
- A paid supervisor can be a losing bidder, a retired contractor, an inspector, or another experienced pro. Class cost sketch (2020, verify): about 1,000 dollars, or 5–10% of rehab, depending on visit count, job size, and permits. A selling realtor can be a second, unpaid, set of eyes if they will list the finished house.
- Release money only per the contract and after supervisor approval.
- If the exit is a flip, the listing agent can start marketing about two weeks before completion.
- After a flip sale, handle title directions, investor payoff, and tax holdbacks with the accountant — do not wire residual profits casually.

## Example defaults (H.S HOLDINGS)
- No final MAO should treat a single unverified guess as a locked rehab number when time remains to get bids.
- Three bids inside the inspection period are the default on a serious contract.
- Flip and BRRRR profit / left-in tests are rerun when the winning bid differs from the underwriting rehab number.

## Outdated items
- Supervisor fee examples (about 1,000 dollars or 5–10%) are 2020 class figures; re-price locally.
- Trade availability, permit times, and material costs have changed since the course (2019–2020).
