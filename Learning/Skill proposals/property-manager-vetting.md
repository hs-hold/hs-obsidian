# Property-manager vetting and oversight checklist

## Why it helps H.S HOLDINGS
Gives a concrete way to interview Indianapolis PMs (even if rentals are self-managed) and a standard for tenant screening, fees, exit rights, and repair conflicts that protect cash flow.

## Suggested runner
Existing bots: the **secretary** can run the interview checklist and file the answers; **Indianapolis Market Research** can help short-list companies in the target zips. **Deal Analysis** uses the rent figure and fee assumptions in the pro forma. A new bot is not required unless PM oversight becomes a monthly operational load (then a small "Asset Desk" could own the reports).

## When to use
- A partner will meet or call property managers.
- A rent opinion is needed during a property inspection period.
- A PM agreement is being reviewed, or monthly reports need a monitoring pass.
- The team is self-managing but wants the same screening and expense standards.

## What a PM is supposed to do (class list)
The PM is the owner's long arm. In the training, that includes:

- During inspection, give a rent opinion for the property.
- Advertise and place a suitable tenant.
- Screen: credit; rental history (class rule of thumb: no eviction in the prior 7 years); crime-record check, as legally allowed in that jurisdiction.
- Sign the lease documents (on the owner's approved form).
- Collect rent, remit to the owner, handle required repairs, and provide tenant customer service.

Screening criteria must be updated against **current fair-housing and local law**. The 7-year eviction example is a 2020 class point, not automatic legal advice.

## Selection criteria
- Number of doors under management (ask advantages and disadvantages of that size).
- Areas of specialization, and whether they actually operate in the subject neighborhood.
- Time in business (class threshold: **at least 3 years**).
- Referral from a realtor, another investor, or a contractor.
- Sample owner reports — ask to see one.
- 24-hour emergency number.
- Online portal to view the owner's account.
- **Critical contract term from the class:** the owner can exit in the first year with **30 days' notice and no penalty**. If this is missing, flag it.

Also useful, from a later business-building class: keep more than one PM relationship in the same market for backup and comparison; visit in person periodically; and watch the conflict that appears when a PM earns more by doing more repairs.

## Typical fee sketch (2020 class — verify locally)
These are teaching figures, not a bid:

- Ongoing management: a percentage of **income, not profit**. Class typical: **about 10% on a single property**, sometimes with a minimum fee.
- New tenant placement: about **3–4 weeks of gross rent**. Operating vacancies and other owner costs still sit with the owner during turnover.
- Renewal with the same tenant: often about **two weeks of gross rent**.
- Sometimes **10% or a minimum of about 30 dollars** on repair invoices, especially if the owner asked the PM to collect three repair bids — read the agreement.
- **Conflict to watch:** some companies use in-house vendors and then have an incentive to do more repairs at higher prices.

Owner controls taught in class: require before-and-after repair photos; occasionally call the tenant to ask if the repair was done well; independently spot-check repair pricing (class named HomeAdvisor and similar — use a current local price source); require reports and deposits on a fixed date; require late fees when owed; tell the PM exactly what to do on non-payment; and remember the owner is the decision maker. Know the signed management agreement and add the clauses you need before signing.

## Owner-level expense reminder (for underwriting)
Class rule of thumb after typical owner costs (management, insurance, property tax, repairs, vacancy, possible eviction, landscaping or snow if not passed through): about **60–65% of rent left as net, before income tax**, with vacancy often sketched at about **5%**. Compare this to the company's own cash-flow model; do not replace a full pro forma.

## Example defaults (H.S HOLDINGS)
- Strategy is self-managed rentals first. Use this skill to (a) interview PMs as a backup and as a rent-comp source, and (b) copy a professional screening and repair-oversight standard if self-managing.
- Underwriting should still clear the current BRRRR cash-flow default (at least 250 dollars per month on the team's current assumptions) after realistic fees or after a self-management time-and-cost allowance.
- Do not sign a PM agreement that traps the owner beyond a 30-day first-year exit unless counsel and partners accept that.

## Outdated items
- 10% / 3–4 weeks / 30-dollar repair markup figures are 2020 class norms; Indianapolis 2026 quotes will differ.
- HomeAdvisor as a price-check site may not be the best current source.
- Tenant-screening rules have changed in some cities and states; verify legality before applying a fixed eviction-lookback or crime-record rule.
