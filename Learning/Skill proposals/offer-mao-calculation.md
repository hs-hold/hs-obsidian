# Offer and MAO calculation checklist

## Why it helps H.S HOLDINGS
Keeps every offer inside a written MAO, shown next to the asking price, so partners do not bid into a thin flip or a BRRRR that leaves too much in after refinance.

## Suggested runner
Existing bot: **Deal Analysis**. Indianapolis / Atlanta Market Research and Zip Intel supply comps and rents. Contract Desk is not the calculator. No new bot needed.

## When to use
- A new lead, MLS listing, wholesaler sheet, or FSBO arrives and needs a number.
- A partner asks "what can we offer?" or "is this a deal?"
- An existing offer is being revised after new rehab, rent, or comp data.

Do not use this skill to draft contract language (see `purchase-contract-protections`) or to run seller/wholesaler calls (see `seller-and-wholesaler-call-scripts`).

## Four numbers required before any MAO
Collect all four, even if the strategy is a flip (rent is plan B):

1. After-repair value (ARV), from recent sold comps, not the asking price.
2. Rehab cost, from a contractor or a clearly labeled rough estimate (roof-to-floor). Flag rough estimates as unverified.
3. Asking price and the offer being considered.
4. Market rent (Rentometer, listings, a property manager, or a realtor).

If any of the four is missing, say so and do not invent a precise MAO.

## Process
1. Confirm the exit path first (flip, BRRRR/rental, or a stated backup). A deal is not complete until an exit is named.
2. Build ARV from comps; say why each comp was kept or dropped.
3. Build rehab from scope, not a round guess. If only a rough number exists, show a range and the effect on MAO.
4. Compute MAO for the chosen exit, then also compute the other exit as a check.
5. Always display **MAO next to asking price**, plus the spread. Do not add extra hidden conservatism layers on top of the stated rules unless a partner asks for a sensitivity case.
6. An offer should be at or below MAO. The course material states that most offers are negotiable; the cap is the MAO, not the ask.
7. Speed target from the source training: a first-pass analysis plus an initial offer figure should be possible in about 10–15 minutes once the four numbers exist. That is a first pass, not a substitute for inspection.

## Formulas and course benchmarks (mark as dated / compare, do not auto-replace current rules)
These come from Project X Israel and Cycle 28 deal-day material (teaching examples, roughly 2019–2020). They are teaching benchmarks, not current company policy.

- A property is a "deal" only if it is enough below market to leave room for profit after friction. The course notes that a U.S. sale commission is often around 6%, so **5–7% below market is usually not a deal**.
- Course rental sketch: estimate expenses at about **45% of gross rent**, then back into a max purchase from a target yield (a class example used 10%). Use only as a side-by-side check.
- Course flip sketch (class example only): in one lesson, 25% of ARV was reserved as profit: ARV 160,000, rehab 30,000, max purchase 90,000. Other spoken examples used bands such as **12–20% below market** for a flip (sometimes up to 25%) and roughly **8–15%** or a 12–20% band for a rental buy. There is no single magic discount.
- Health metric used in class: how many offers were submitted in the last week. A week with zero offers means nothing is moving.

If the course sketch and the company's current rules disagree, **show both and follow the company's current rules**.

## Example defaults (H.S HOLDINGS current rules — replace if the partners update them)
Label these as company defaults, not universal investor truth:

- Preferred ARV band: about 200,000–300,000.
- Active market: Indianapolis. Atlanta is paused unless a partner reopens it.
- Flip: target net profit about 20,000–25,000 or more.
- BRRRR: refinance at 65% LTV, at most 60,000 left in after the refi, cash flow at least 250 per month (self-managed assumption unless stated otherwise).
- Always show MAO next to asking price. Do not stack extra cushion beyond these rules unless asked.

## Outputs the runner should always include
- Strategy and backup exit.
- ARV, rehab, rent, ask, MAO, and ask-vs-MAO spread.
- Whether the deal clears the stated defaults, and which input would break it.
- What still has to be verified (comps, contractor bid, rent, taxes, insurance).

## Outdated or easy-to-misuse items
- 2019–2020 example prices and percentage bands are teaching illustrations, not 2026 Indianapolis comps.
- The 45% expense ratio and 10% yield example are course shortcuts, not a substitute for a full operating pro forma (taxes, insurance, vacancy, maintenance, management).
