# Purchase-contract protections checklist

## Why it helps H.S HOLDINGS
Stops the company from signing a 2019-style off-market form or a wholesaler assignment that is missing inspection exit rights, refundable earnest money, assignability, or the underlying owner contract.

## Suggested runner
Existing bot: **Contract Desk** owns the review. Deal Analysis supplies the price and dates; it does not approve legal language. A local real-estate attorney remains the final legal check. No new bot needed.

## When to use
- Any draft purchase agreement, including a short off-market form, a realtor form, or a wholesaler contract.
- A partner asks "can we sign this?" or "what's missing?"
- Before earnest money is sent.

This skill is a diligence checklist from investor training. It is **not legal advice** and does not replace counsel in the property's state.

## Hard rules from the training
- A written offer is not a completed purchase. You need at least one clean exit, usually an inspection window.
- Do not recommend signing if the checklist below fails, unless a partner explicitly accepts a named risk after counsel's view.
- On a wholesaler deal, require **two** contracts: the original owner contract and the assignment. The assignment should describe the wholesaler's fee. Read the underlying terms — you step into that contract.

## Checklist
Review and write a pass / fail / missing note for each item:

1. **Parties and assignability.** Buyer named "and/or assignees" or an equivalent assignment clause. Successors and assignees are bound. If assignment is absent, flag it.
2. **Property identification.** Address, parcel / tax ID, land, improvements, fixtures, and any personal property included.
3. **Price and payment.** Purchase price is clear. State who pays closing costs, unpaid taxes, mortgages, and liens. Do not assume the buyer pays everything unless that was a deliberate offer term. Class default: costs are often split; do not silently load all costs onto the buyer.
4. **Earnest money.** Amount stated. Prefer small and clearly **refundable** through the inspection period, in writing, without a seller-approval trap. Class examples: 0, or about 500–1,000 dollars (sometimes 2,000). If the buyer later defaults after the exit window, liquidated damages may be limited to the deposit (as in the 2019 sample form) — confirm state law.
5. **Inspection / due-diligence period.** Calendar days are numbered. Class guidance: **at least 14 days, preferably more**. A 2019 off-market sample used **25 calendar days** from the last signature, termination in the **buyer's sole discretion**, with written notice, and seller cooperation on access. A useful teaching phrase: contingent on "buyer's partners' inspection and approval" at the partners' sole discretion until the inspection period ends, with earnest money refundable until then. One clear exit is enough; financing contingency is optional if inspection exit is solid.
6. **Closing and title.** Closing date (a sample used 30 calendar days from last signature). Possession at closing unless a negotiated occupancy agreement exists. Marketable title, listed exceptions only, title insurance. Who provides the policy is a negotiated point (the 2019 sample had the seller furnish it — confirm local custom). Warranty deed / clean title language should be present.
7. **Prorations and deposits.** Taxes, rents, insurance, interest prorated at closing. Tenant security deposits transferred. Impound shortfalls charged to the seller if those accounts transfer.
8. **Condition until closing.** Seller maintains and insures the property. If insured damage occurs, the buyer may have the option to close and take proceeds (sample form). Walk-through one to two days before closing.
9. **Lead paint.** If the home was built before 1978, required U.S. disclosures must be present.
10. **"As-is"** does not cancel inspection or termination rights. Flag any as-is clause that tries to do so.
11. **Access, advertising, early repairs.** If the form allows pre-closing access, showing, or buyer-paid repairs, flag the default risk (sample form: improvements stay with the seller if the buyer defaults).
12. **Agency / exclusivity with a realtor.** Any buyer-agency exclusive should be limited to a specific property (not an entire area) and a short term. Class guidance: a modest flat fee in the hundreds, not an open-ended area exclusive.
13. **Dates and notices.** How termination must be delivered, and by when.
14. **Entire agreement / counsel.** The form should say it is binding and that a party who does not understand it should seek legal advice.

## Sample off-market form (2019) — use as a teaching specimen only
The Cycle 28 "Off_Market purchase agreement" is a short 2019 cash form: 100% cash at closing, 30-day close, 25-day sole-discretion inspection, assignable, seller to furnish title insurance, buyer pays closing costs minus unpaid taxes/mortgage/liens. Have counsel adapt it for the current state and year. Do not send the 2019 specimen as-is.

## Example defaults (H.S HOLDINGS)
- Inspection: at least 14 days, preferably more, with partner-approval / sole-discretion exit and refundable earnest money.
- Earnest money: prefer 0–1,000 dollars unless a specific deal requires more, always refundable through inspection.
- Price in the contract must be at or below the MAO from `offer-mao-calculation`.
- Indianapolis / Indiana counsel should review before first use of any template, especially assignment language.

## Outdated or high-risk items
- 2019 form dates, cash-only structure, and "buyer pays all closing costs" allocation may not match 2026 Indiana custom or the company's preferred deal.
- Wholesaling and assignment rules have tightened in some states since the course; confirm Indiana current law with counsel.
- E-sign tools named in class (DocuSign, HelloSign) are interchangeable only if the team already uses them securely.
